Depression

Depression is a common mental health condition with a persistent low mood, loss of interest or pleasure, low energy, and changes in sleep, appetite, thinking and behaviour. It is more than normal sadness because it lasts and gets in the way of daily life.

Causes and risk factors

Biological

Genetics and changes in brain chemicals and hormones such as dopamine and serotonin.

Psychological

Low self-esteem, negative thinking and trauma.

Social

Bereavement, relationship problems, money worries and isolation.

Medical

Hypothyroidism, chronic diseases, neurological disorders and some medications.

Major symptoms: remember SIGECAPS

  • SSleep disturbance
  • ILoss of interest or pleasure
  • GGuilt or worthlessness
  • ELow energy
  • CPoor concentration
  • AAppetite or weight changes
  • PPsychomotor agitation or slowing
  • SSuicidal thoughts

A major depressive episode generally involves 5 or more of these symptoms for at least 2 weeks, including low mood or loss of interest or pleasure.

Types, assessment and management

Types of depression
  • Major depressive disorder
  • Persistent depressive disorder (dysthymia)
  • Bipolar depression
  • Seasonal-pattern depression
  • Postpartum depression
  • Psychotic depression
How it is assessed
  • History of symptoms and how long they have lasted
  • Mental-status examination
  • Medical and medication history
  • Substance-use assessment
  • Screening tools such as the PHQ-9
  • Suicide-risk assessment
  • Checking for past mania or hypomania to rule out bipolar disorder
How it is managed
  • Psychotherapy, especially CBT, interpersonal therapy and behavioural activation
  • TMS (transcranial magnetic stimulation) in selected cases
  • Exercise, enough sleep, healthy food and social support
  • Avoiding alcohol and recreational drugs

How to help a friend who is depressed

  1. Check for suicide or self-harm risk, and protect them.
  2. Encourage them to express their feelings.
  3. Keep an eye on their food, sleep and activity.
  4. Help with daily tasks when they need it.
  5. Encourage healthy social contact.

The key point

Depression is treatable, but severe depression can be life-threatening because of the risk of suicide. Early recognition, the right treatment, close monitoring and support from people around you all matter.

If someone is in danger, act now

Call your local emergency number or go to the nearest hospital. Stay with the person until help arrives.
