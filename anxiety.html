Anxiety

Anxiety is a normal, useful response. It keeps you alert before interviews, exams and appointments. It becomes a problem when it is too strong, lasts too long, or you can’t cope with it.

The four levels of anxiety

Mild anxiety

  • Increases alertness and awareness of your surroundings
  • Improves concentration, learning, problem-solving and motivation

Example: anxiety pushes a student to study for an exam.

Moderate anxiety

  • You feel something is wrong and become nervous or agitated
  • Concentration is harder, but you can still learn and solve problems with help
  • Your attention can be redirected

Example: an anxious patient receiving instructions before an operation.

Severe anxiety

  • Thinking, reasoning and concentration are badly affected
  • You may be restless, irritable, angry or pacing
  • Muscle tension and vital signs rise

Panic

  • The most severe level, where fight, flight or freeze takes over
  • Adrenaline surges, raising heart rate and dilating the pupils
  • Thinking is severely impaired and attention narrows to survival

Signs and symptoms

Physical

  • Fast heartbeat or palpitations
  • Sweating, trembling
  • Rapid breathing
  • Muscle tension, headache
  • Dizziness, nausea
  • Restlessness, poor sleep

Psychological

  • Excessive worry or fear
  • Feeling nervous or tense
  • Irritability
  • Trouble concentrating
  • Racing thoughts
  • Feeling overwhelmed

Behavioural

  • Avoiding people or situations
  • Pacing or fidgeting
  • Difficulty sitting still
  • Repeatedly seeking reassurance
  • Struggling with normal activities

What can help with mild to moderate anxiety

Calm the body

Breathe in for 4 seconds and out for 6. Try muscle relaxation, meditation or mindfulness. Use the breathing guide.

Look after basics

Exercise regularly, keep a steady sleep routine, eat balanced meals, drink water, and cut back on caffeine and energy drinks.

Connect and plan

Talk to someone you trust. Break big tasks into small steps. Make time for music, hobbies, prayer or time in nature.

Avoid alcohol and recreational drugs. They may ease anxiety for a short time but make it worse over time.

Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT)

CBT is a talking therapy that helps you spot and change the negative thoughts, feelings and behaviours that feed anxiety and other mental health problems.
